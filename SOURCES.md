# Sources

Every science figure in You Are Here comes from one of the sources below. Figures that change over time, such as moon counts, carry an "as of" date. Where a value is a modelling or drawing choice rather than a measurement, the code says so.

## Positions, time and frames

- **[Astronomy Engine](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy)** 2.1.19 (Don Cross, MIT). Positions of the Sun, the Moon, the planets and Pluto, accurate to within about 1 arcminute; Moon phases; solar and lunar eclipse predictions; IAU rotation models (tilt and spin) for the Sun, the Moon and the planets; rotations between the J2000 equator, the J2000 ecliptic and the galactic frame; constellation boundaries; the positions of Jupiter's four largest moons; equinoxes, solstices, oppositions, conjunctions and transits.
- The clock runs from 1900 to 2100. Earth's city lights are modern satellite data (2016), so far from today the night side shows today's lights.

## The Milky Way

- **Spiral arms:** Reid, M. J. et al. 2019, ApJ 885, 131, ["Trigonometric Parallaxes of High-mass Star-forming Regions: Our View of the Milky Way"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03357), Table 2 (log-periodic spiral fits); also the Sun's distance from the centre, R0 = 8.15 ± 0.15 kpc (about 26,600 light-years), and the Sun's circular speed, 247 ± 4 km/s.
- **Central bar:** half-length 5.0 ± 0.2 kpc at 28–33° to the Sun–centre line, [Wegg, Gerhard & Portail 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.01401); bulge scale lengths and bar height, [Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07702). The bar's length is still debated (about 3.5 to 5 kpc).
- **Disc:** scale length 2.6 kpc (Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016); thin-disc scale height about 300 pc ([Wikipedia, Thin disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thin_disk)).
- **Arm descriptions:** [NASA, The Milky Way Galaxy](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/the-milky-way-galaxy/); [NASA/JPL 2008](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/two-of-the-milky-ways-spiral-arms-go-missing/).
- **Orion Arm:** about 3,500 light-years wide and 20,000 long, with the Sun near its inner edge ([Wikipedia, Orion Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orion_Arm)).
- **Galactic plane and ecliptic:** about 60° apart ([Swinburne University, Galactic Plane](https://astronomy.swinburne.edu.au/cosmos/G/Galactic+Plane)); checked in the app with Astronomy Engine's rotation matrices.
- **Galactic centre:** Sagittarius A* has 4.297 ± 0.012 million solar masses, and R0 = 8,277 ± 9 pc ([GRAVITY Collaboration 2022, A&A 657, L12](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07478)); GRAVITY 2019 gave 8,178 ± 13 ± 22 pc ([A&A 625, L10](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05721)).

## The Sun, the planets and Pluto

- **[NASA Planetary Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/index.html)** (updated 18 March 2025): diameters, rotation periods, axial tilts, gravity, distances, orbital periods and speeds, mean temperatures. Its [notes](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/planetfact_notes.html) explain that the giant planets' temperature and gravity are given at the 1-bar pressure level.
- **[NASA Sun Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/sunfact.html)** (updated 9 May 2024) and [NASA Sun facts](https://science.nasa.gov/sun/facts/).
- **[NASA Moon Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/moonfact.html)** (updated 11 January 2024).
- **[NASA Earth Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html)** (updated 15 November 2024): Earth's mean orbital speed, 29.78 km/s.
- **Mercury's day and night temperatures:** about 430 °C and −180 °C ([NASA Mercury facts](https://science.nasa.gov/mercury/facts/)).
- **Known moons, as of 9 November 2026:** Jupiter 115, Saturn 293, Uranus 29, Neptune 16, Pluto 5 ([NASA Jupiter moons](https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/moons/), [Saturn moons](https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/), [Uranus moons](https://science.nasa.gov/uranus/moons/), [Neptune moons](https://science.nasa.gov/neptune/moons/), [Pluto moons](https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/pluto/moons/); Minor Planet Center electronic circulars of April and June 2026).
- **Pluto is a dwarf planet:** IAU 2006 Resolutions B5 and B6 ([IAU press release](https://www.iau.org/IAU/Iau/News/PR2006/iau-2006-general-assembly-resolution-votes.aspx)).
- **Worked figures** (arithmetic from the fact sheet): sunlight takes about 8 minutes 19 seconds to reach Earth (149.6 million km ÷ 299,792 km/s); at true scale, if Earth were 1 pixel wide, Neptune would be about 354,000 pixels from the Sun (4,515 million km ÷ 12,756 km).

## Moons, rings and belts

- **Titan, Triton, Charon and the Galilean moons:** orbits from [JPL Planetary Satellite Mean Elements](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/) (simple mean orbits, not for precise ephemerides); radii from [JPL Planetary Satellite Physical Parameters](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/phys_par/). Titan, Triton and Charon are drawn on these simple orbits and labelled "approximate"; Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto come from Astronomy Engine.
- **Saturn's rings:** [NASA Saturnian Rings Fact Sheet](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/satringfact.html) (updated 19 April 2022), in Saturn's equatorial plane.
- **Main asteroid belt:** JPL's main-belt class, 2.0 to 3.2 au ([JPL SBDB](https://ssd-api.jpl.nasa.gov/doc/sbdb_filter.html)); Kirkwood gaps most prominent at 2.5 and 2.8 au (DeMeo & Carry 2014, Nature 505, 629).
- **Kuiper belt:** from Neptune's orbit at about 30 AU to about 50 AU ([NASA Kuiper Belt facts](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/kuiper-belt/facts/)); main belt between the 3:2 and 2:1 resonances, 39.4 to 47.7 AU (Gladman & Volk 2021, ARA&A 59, 203).
- The particle density of both belts is illustrative.

## Story features

- **Pale Blue Dot:** Voyager 1 took the Earth frames at 04:48 GMT on 14 February 1990 ([NASA](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/voyager-1s-pale-blue-dot/)). Its position, from [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/) (target −31, heliocentric, J2000 ecliptic): x = −15.742, y = −30.058, z = +21.857 AU, 40.36 AU (6.04 billion km) from the Sun and 32.8° above the ecliptic. Carl Sagan's words about the photo: [The Planetary Society](https://www.planetary.org/worlds/pale-blue-dot).
- **Galaxy landmarks** (positions in galactic longitude and latitude from [SIMBAD](https://simbad.cds.unistra.fr)): Orion Nebula 388 ± 5 pc ([Kounkel et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.04041)); Pleiades 136.2 ± 1.2 pc ([Melis et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.6544)); Hyades about 47 pc (Gaia DR3, Hunt & Reffert 2023); Crab Nebula 1.90 +0.22/−0.18 kpc ([Lin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01617)); Eagle Nebula about 1.7 kpc and Lagoon Nebula 1.23–1.34 kpc (Gaia DR3/DR2); Carina Nebula 2.35 ± 0.05 kpc ([Göppl & Preibisch 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09097)); Omega Centauri 5.43 ± 0.05 kpc ([Baumgardt & Vasiliev 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.09526)); the galactic centre (GRAVITY, above).
- **"You are moving":** Earth around the Sun at 29.78 km/s (NASA Earth Fact Sheet); the Sun around the galaxy at about 247 km/s (Reid et al. 2019).
